## 1. Company overview

Meridian Consumer Group, Inc. (MCG) is an omnichannel consumer-products company founded in 2014 and headquartered in Lakeside City. The group designs, sources, distributes, and sells home, seasonal, and consumer-electronics products under three brands:

| Brand | Product line | Position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harbor | Home Essentials | Practical everyday home goods |
| Terra | Seasonal Living | Outdoor, garden, and seasonal products |
| Lumen | Consumer Electronics | Accessible connected devices |

MCG sells through three channels: **Retail Stores** (its own and partner stores), **E-commerce** (its own site and marketplaces), and **Wholesale** (retail partners and distributors). It operates four regional distribution centres: **North DC**, **South DC**, **East DC**, and **West DC**.

The group is organised into nine functions: Sales, Marketing, Operations, Customer Support, Finance, HR, IT, Legal, and Engineering. Regional directors coordinate commercial activity across North, South, East, and West. Enterprise support functions are centralised.

## 5. Inventory position

The inventory extract is a **sample of 18 SKUs across four distribution centres** (72 SKU–warehouse combinations). It is not the full inventory ledger, but it is sufficient to see the pattern.

| Stock status | Lines | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below Safety Stock | 13 | 18.1% |
| Below Reorder Point | 37 | 51.4% |
| Healthy | 10 | 13.9% |
| Overstock | 12 | 16.7% |

In total, **50 of 72 lines are below reorder point or below safety stock**. Consumer Electronics has 22 of 24 lines at risk and Seasonal Living has 12 overstocked lines. The problem is not simply "too little inventory" or "too much inventory": the company has the wrong inventory in the wrong places.

## 8. Current management agenda

The management committee has identified six priorities for the next 90 days:

1. Stabilise revenue in Retail Stores and the North and East regions.
2. Fix inventory availability in Consumer Electronics while reducing Seasonal Living overstock.
3. Restore customer service levels, especially response time and delivery reliability.
4. Bring the operating cost base back within budget without damaging customer-facing capacity.
5. Address people-measurement issues: rating calibration, overtime, training, and attrition.
6. Strengthen cross-functional governance and unblock the inventory data programme.

These priorities are context for the student's own management instructions. They are not a prescribed answer.

The matrix is effective for scale but creates clear coordination risks at the hand-off points: demand planning, inventory allocation, customer promises, and project ownership.
