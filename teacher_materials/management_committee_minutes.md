# Management Committee Minutes

**Meridian Consumer Group, Inc.**
**Meeting date:** 28 September 2026
**Location:** Lakeside City headquarters / video conference
**Chair:** Chief Executive Officer
**Attendees:** CFO, COO, CHRO, CMO, CIO, VP Sales, VP Customer Support, General Counsel
**Minutes owner:** Chief of Staff

## 1. Purpose

The meeting reviewed the Q3 2026 trading position, inventory and service performance, the people agenda, and the delivery of cross-functional projects. The committee agreed a 90-day recovery focus and assigned actions.

## 2. Q3 trading update

The CFO reported:

- Q3 2026 revenue was **$6.60 billion**, down **11.8%** on Q3 2025 and **16.0%** below target.
- Gross margin fell to **32.86%** from 34.60%.
- Q3 operating expenses were **$2.79 billion**, producing an operating loss of approximately **$618 million**.
- 2026 year-to-date operating expenses are **$156.6 million above budget**.
- The largest unfavourable variances are logistics (+$52.9 million), technology (+$42.2 million), marketing (+$39.0 million), customer support (+$33.9 million), and professional services (+$12.3 million).

The VP Sales reported:

- Retail Stores revenue fell **19.6%** year on year.
- E-commerce grew **3.3%**, but not enough to offset the retail decline.
- Wholesale fell **12.2%**.
- Consumer Electronics fell **17.4%**, Seasonal Living fell **14.2%**, and Home Essentials fell **7.3%**.
- North and East were the weakest regions, down 15.8% and 15.5% respectively.

The committee noted that the decline is not a single-channel or single-product problem. It combines channel mix, availability, regional execution, and cost pressure.

## 3. Inventory and supply

The COO reported:

- In the provided sample of 72 SKU–warehouse lines, **50 are below reorder point or safety stock**.
- Consumer Electronics has **22 of 24 lines at risk**.
- Seasonal Living has **12 overstocked lines**.
- The inventory data cleanup task (T-020) is blocked and past due, which weakens confidence in replenishment decisions.
- Expedited freight is being used to protect customer orders, but logistics costs are now 10.2% over budget.

The committee agreed that the inventory problem is a **placement and data problem**, not simply a total-inventory problem. The wrong stock is in the wrong DCs, and the master-data ownership is unclear.

## 4. Customer experience

The VP Customer Support reported:

- CSAT fell from **80.4 in 2025** to **71.5 in 2026 year to date**.
- NPS fell from **31.0 to 14.1**.
- Complaints rose from **32,071** in 2025 to **47,199** in 2026 year to date.
- Average response time rose from **6.1 hours to 11.3 hours**.
- The main complaint themes are late delivery, product unavailable, support response time, damaged items, and refund delays.
- The customer response time reduction task (T-004) is blocked and due on 30 September.

The committee agreed that service recovery must be linked to inventory availability and delivery promises. Adding support capacity without fixing fulfilment will not restore CSAT.

## 5. People agenda

The CHRO reported:

- Active headcount is **89,990**; the separation flag rate is **10.01%**.
- Average overtime is **14.5 hours** per week, above the 10-hour policy target.
- Average training is **49.5 hours**, below the 60-hour target.
- Performance ratings are almost perfectly uniform: 18,086 employees at 1, 18,021 at 2, 17,980 at 3, 17,909 at 4, and 17,994 at 5.
- Department-level performance and satisfaction averages are very similar.
- The performance rating calibration task (T-006) is in progress but only 20% complete.
- The Q4 seasonal staffing plan (T-026) is in progress.

The committee agreed that the uniform rating distribution is a **measurement and manager-capability issue**. It does not support differentiated talent decisions. Calibration and first-line manager coaching are priorities, not administrative extras.

## 6. Project and governance position

The committee reviewed the task register:

- 17 tasks are in progress.
- 8 have not started.
- 3 are blocked: T-004 (customer response time), T-008 (ERP procurement module), and T-020 (inventory data cleanup).
- 2 are complete.
- T-020 is past due.
- The register has an approved budget of **$4.07 million** and **$1.83 million** spent.

The committee noted that the blocked tasks share a common cause: unclear decision rights and dependency ownership across functions. The existing escalation route is not being used consistently.

## 7. Decisions

1. **Approve a 90-day recovery plan** with six workstreams: revenue recovery, inventory availability, customer service, cost control, people and capability, and governance.
2. **Use the existing S&OP and Monthly Operating Review** as the control forums. Do not create parallel reporting structures.
3. **Protect customer-facing capacity.** A selective hiring freeze remains in place, with exceptions for Customer Support and warehouse roles approved by the COO and CHRO.
4. **Review marketing spend.** No new campaign above $50,000 may be committed without CFO approval until the effectiveness review is complete.
5. **Tighten discount governance.** Discounts above 15% require Finance approval; seasonal clearance plans require a margin and cash analysis.
6. **Fix inventory data ownership.** The COO and CIO will name a single business owner and a single technical owner for inventory master data by 9 October 2026.
7. **Complete performance calibration by 30 November 2026.** The CHRO will report progress at each monthly operating review.
8. **Restore the customer response standard** to 8 working hours. The VP Customer Support will present a recovery plan by 9 October 2026.
9. **Escalate blocked tasks.** Any cross-functional task blocked for more than 30 days goes to the Executive Committee with a named decision owner.
10. **Report progress** at the next Management Committee meeting on 26 October 2026.
