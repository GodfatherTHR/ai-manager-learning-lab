# Meridian Consumer Group — Operations Manual

**Version 6.1 · Owner: Chief Operating Officer · Applies to Supply Chain, Distribution, Stores, and Customer Support**

## 1. Purpose and scope

This manual describes the standard operating procedures used to plan demand, replenish inventory, fulfil customer orders, handle returns, and manage service performance. It defines the operating measures that managers review and the decision rules that apply when performance falls outside threshold.

The manual is company context. Students may apply their own management instructions to interpret it; the manual does not replace those instructions.

## 2. Planning and S&OP

### 2.1 Weekly cycle

| Day | Activity | Owner | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Demand review: sales, promotions, channel signals | Sales, Marketing, Operations | Updated demand view |
| Tuesday | Supply review: inventory, lead times, capacity | Operations, Procurement | Supply constraints |
| Wednesday | Reconciliation: demand versus supply | Operations, Finance | Recommended plan |
| Thursday | S&OP decision meeting | COO | Agreed plan and escalations |
| Friday | Publish plan and actions | Operations | Commitments and owners |

### 2.2 Planning rules

- The demand plan is unconstrained at the first review; supply constraints are applied in reconciliation.
- Promotions above $100,000 require a demand uplift estimate and an inventory plan.
- New product launches require a launch plan with a stop/go review.
- Forecast accuracy is measured at the product-line and region level.
- A forecast error above 25% for two consecutive months triggers a root-cause review.

## 3. Inventory and replenishment

### 3.1 Inventory measures

| Measure | Definition | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks of supply | Units on hand ÷ average weekly demand | 3–6 weeks for core lines |
| Safety stock | Buffer held to cover demand and lead-time variability | Defined per SKU–DC |
| Reorder point | Demand during lead time plus safety stock | Defined per SKU–DC |
| Stockout risk | On hand + on order below reorder point | Less than 5% of lines |
| Overstock | More than 9 weeks of supply | Action plan required |
| Inventory accuracy | Counted quantity vs system quantity | At least 98% |
| Return rate | Units returned ÷ units sold | Below 5% by category |

### 3.2 Replenishment decision rules

1. If **on hand + on order < reorder point**, the replenishment owner raises an order or transfer within one working day.
2. If **on hand < safety stock**, the category manager reviews expedite options and customer commitments.
3. If a line has **more than 9 weeks of supply**, the owner prepares a clearance, transfer, or markdown plan.
4. If an item is **unavailable for a confirmed order**, Customer Support must offer a substitute, partial shipment, or refund within 24 hours.
5. If a supplier misses a confirmed lead time by more than 5 working days, Procurement must escalate and update the demand plan.
6. If inventory data is disputed, the DC manager and the data owner must reconcile before orders are released.

### 3.3 Inventory accuracy

- Cycle counts are performed daily on a rotating sample and monthly on high-value SKUs.
- A variance above 2% of counted units is investigated.
- A variance above 5% requires a root-cause note and a control action.
- Inventory adjustments above $25,000 require Finance approval.
- The inventory report must state its as-of date and source.

## 4. Distribution and fulfilment

### 4.1 Order fulfilment standards

| Standard | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Orders released within 24 hours of confirmation | At least 95% |
| On-time dispatch | At least 95% |
| On-time delivery | At least 92% |
| Perfect order rate | At least 90% |
| Split shipments | Below 8% of orders |
| Expedited freight | Below 5% of freight spend |

### 4.2 Fulfilment rules

- Orders are allocated to the DC with available stock and the lowest total cost to serve.
- A split shipment requires a cost check and a customer communication.
- Expedited freight requires a documented reason: customer commitment, stockout recovery, or service recovery.
- A DC operating above 90% capacity must escalate to the COO for a capacity plan.
- Safety incidents stop the task and are reported immediately.

## 5. Customer support and service recovery

### 5.1 Service standards

| Measure | Target |
| --- | --- |
| First response time | Within 8 working hours |
| Standard resolution | Within 3 working days |
| Complex resolution | Within 7 working days |
| Refund processing | Within 5 working days of approval |
| Escalation after unresolved complaint | 7 days |
| Repeat complaint review | Within 30 days |
| CSAT | At least 80 |
| NPS | At least 30 |

### 5.2 Service recovery rules

1. Acknowledge the customer's issue and confirm the next action.
2. Check the order, inventory, and delivery status before promising a resolution.
3. If the item is unavailable, offer a substitute, partial shipment, or refund.
4. If the complaint is caused by a company process failure, record the root cause and escalate.
5. A complaint unresolved after 7 days goes to the duty manager.
6. A repeated complaint within 30 days goes to service quality.
7. Goodwill credits follow the decision-rights table in `company_policies.md`.

### 5.3 Returns and refunds

- Returns require a reason code.
- Electronics returns require a quality check.
- A category return rate above 5% requires a root-cause review.
- Refund delays are treated as service failures and reviewed weekly.
- Return data is shared with Product, Operations, and Finance.

## 6. Store operations

- Store labour is scheduled to demand, service standards, and legal requirements.
- A store with sustained overtime above 12 hours per employee per week must review its schedule.
- Stockouts in a store are reported daily and reviewed in the weekly S&OP.
- Store managers may use local recovery actions within their approval limits.
- Visual merchandising and promotional execution are checked weekly.

## 7. People and capability in operations

- Every DC and store has a named safety lead.
- First-line managers complete people-leadership training within 90 days of appointment.
- Overtime and attendance are reviewed monthly.
- Cross-training is planned for critical roles to reduce single-person dependency.
- Seasonal labour is planned at least six weeks before the peak.
- A team with attrition above 12% requires a retention plan.

## 8. Operational governance

### 8.1 Daily

- DC and store performance huddle: safety, service, stockouts, labour.
- Customer support huddle: backlog, escalations, response time.

### 8.2 Weekly

- S&OP: demand, supply, inventory, service.
- Operations review: cost, productivity, safety, accuracy.
- Customer service review: complaints, root causes, recovery actions.

### 8.3 Monthly

- Monthly Operating Review: financial, customer, people, projects.
- Inventory accuracy review.
- Supplier performance review.
- Project stage gates.

## 9. Escalation matrix

| Trigger | First escalation | Timeframe | Next escalation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Safety incident | DC/store manager | Immediately | COO |
| Stockout on a confirmed order | Category manager | Same day | VP Operations |
| Customer unresolved after 7 days | Duty manager | Same day | VP Customer Support |
| Supplier miss above 5 days | Procurement | Same day | COO |
| Blocked cross-functional task | Task owner | 5 working days | COO |
| Opex category above 5% of budget | Budget owner | Monthly review | CFO |
| Project milestone missed | Project sponsor | Stage gate | Executive Committee |
| Data integrity issue | Data owner | Same day | CIO and COO |

## 10. Management cadence and measures

The operating model uses a small set of measures to avoid distraction:

- Revenue versus plan
- Gross margin
- Operating expense versus budget
- On-time delivery and perfect order rate
- Inventory weeks of supply, stockout risk, and overstock
- CSAT, NPS, response time, and complaints
- Overtime, attrition, training, and performance-rating calibration
- Project on-time delivery and blocked tasks

Measures must be reviewed with their source, as-of date, and owner. A measure without an owner is not a control.
