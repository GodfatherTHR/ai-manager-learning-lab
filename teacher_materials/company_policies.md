# Meridian Consumer Group — Company Policies

**Version 4.2 · Effective 1 January 2026 · Owner: Chief Operating Officer and Chief Human Resources Officer**

This document sets the formal policies and decision rights that managers are expected to follow. It is company context, not a substitute for the student's own management instructions.

## 1. Planning and performance management

### 1.1 Planning cycle

- The annual operating plan is approved by the Executive Committee in November for the following calendar year.
- Each function and region submits a quarterly forecast by the fifth working day of the quarter.
- Finance consolidates the forecast into a monthly rolling view.
- The Sales & Operations Planning (S&OP) meeting reviews demand, supply, inventory, and service capacity every Monday.
- The Monthly Operating Review (MOR) reviews financial performance, people metrics, customer metrics, and the action register.

### 1.2 Targets and variance thresholds

| Measure | Target or threshold | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue vs plan | 100% | Any gap greater than 3% requires a recovery action with an owner and date |
| Gross margin | At least 34% | Any gap greater than 1 point requires a pricing, mix, or cost review |
| Operating expense vs budget | 100% or better | Any category more than 5% over budget requires CFO review |
| CSAT | At least 80 | A quarterly decline of 5 points or more triggers a service recovery plan |
| NPS | At least 30 | A decline of 10 points or more triggers root-cause analysis |
| Stock below reorder point | Less than 5% of SKU–DC lines | DC and category managers must present a replenishment plan |
| Weeks of supply | 3–6 weeks for core lines | Above 9 weeks is treated as overstock and requires an action plan |
| Overtime | Average no more than 10 hours per employee per week | Above 12 hours requires a manager action plan |
| Attrition | No more than 10% | A department above 12% requires an HR review |
| Training | At least 60 hours per employee per year | A department below 50 hours requires a capability plan |
| Project on-time delivery | At least 85% | Any blocked task older than 30 days escalates to the Executive Committee |

### 1.3 Corrective action

A variance outside threshold requires a written corrective action with:

1. The measurable gap and its cause.
2. The action owner and completion date.
3. The expected financial or service effect.
4. The leading indicator that will show whether the action is working.
5. The escalation point if the action is not delivered.

## 2. Organisation and delegation

### 2.1 Decision rights

| Decision | Manager | Director | Functional VP | CFO / COO | CEO / Board |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operating expense commitment | Up to $25,000 | Up to $100,000 | Up to $500,000 | Up to $2,000,000 | Above $2,000,000 |
| Inventory markdown | Up to 10% | Up to 20% | Up to 30% | — | Above 30% |
| New hire (approved headcount) | — | Recommends | Approves | — | — |
| New hire (outside approved headcount) | — | — | Recommends | Approves exception | CEO for VP+ roles |
| Supplier selection | — | Recommends | Approves | Approves contracts above $1,000,000 | Board if strategic |
| Customer goodwill credit | Up to $500 | Up to $2,500 | Up to $10,000 | — | Above $10,000 |
| Policy exception | — | — | Recommends | Approves | Board if material |

### 2.2 Delegation rules

- Delegation must be specific: task, decision authority, deadline, and escalation route.
- A manager may delegate a task but remains accountable for the outcome.
- Decisions above a manager's authority must be escalated before commitment, not after.
- A delegate must have the competence, information, and resources needed to act.
- Urgent operational decisions may be taken by the duty manager overnight, but must be documented and reviewed the next working day.

### 2.3 Cross-functional coordination

- Demand planning is owned by Operations with input from Sales, Marketing, and Finance.
- Inventory master data is jointly owned by Operations and IT; business ownership sits with the COO.
- Customer promises are owned by Customer Support, with fulfilment commitments from Operations.
- Project funding is owned by Finance; project prioritisation is owned by the Executive Committee.
- Any cross-functional task with no named owner is escalated to the COO within five working days.

## 3. Staffing and people

### 3.1 Workforce planning

- All hiring must be against an approved headcount line in the annual plan.
- Customer-facing and warehouse roles may be exempted from a general hiring freeze with COO and CHRO approval.
- Contingent labour may be used for seasonal peaks but must be included in the monthly cost forecast.
- Every open role must have a named hiring manager and a target fill date.

### 3.2 Performance management

- Performance reviews use a five-point scale: 1 = does not meet expectations, 5 = exceptional.
- Ratings must be supported by documented evidence, not by a single manager's impression.
- Managers complete calibration before ratings are finalised. The calibration objective is a defensible distribution, not a forced ranking.
- A rating distribution that is almost perfectly uniform across a large population is a signal to review the quality of goal-setting and assessment.
- Employees with a rating of 1 or 2 require a written improvement plan within 10 working days.
- High performers (rating 4 or 5) must be considered for development, retention, and succession actions.

### 3.3 Learning and development

- Every employee should complete at least 60 training hours per year.
- First-line managers complete at least 20 hours of people-leadership training.
- Training completion is reviewed monthly by the CHRO and reported in the MOR.
- A department below 50 average training hours must submit a capability plan.

### 3.4 Overtime, wellbeing, and attendance

- Overtime must be approved in advance by the line manager.
- Sustained overtime above 12 hours per week requires a workload review.
- Sick leave is monitored for patterns; it must not be used as a disciplinary measure without HR review.
- Remote work is role-dependent. Teams may use up to 50% remote work where service, security, and collaboration standards are met.
- Employees must be able to raise concerns without retaliation.

### 3.5 Attrition and retention

- Exit interviews are required for voluntary departures.
- A department with attrition above 12% must present a retention plan.
- Critical-role turnover is reviewed separately from overall turnover.
- Retention actions must be evidence-based and cannot rely only on pay.

## 4. Customer and service policies

### 4.1 Service standards

- First response to a customer complaint: within 8 working hours.
- Resolution target: within 3 working days for standard cases; 7 working days for complex cases.
- Refunds are processed within 5 working days of approval.
- A complaint that remains unresolved after 7 days escalates to the Customer Support duty manager.
- A repeat complaint from the same customer within 30 days escalates to the service quality team.

### 4.2 Product availability and substitution

- If an ordered item is unavailable, the customer must be offered a substitute, a partial shipment, or a refund within 24 hours.
- Substitution must not reduce the customer's value without consent.
- Repeated stockouts of the same SKU are reviewed in the weekly S&OP meeting.
- Customer-facing teams must not promise a delivery date that Operations has not confirmed.

### 4.3 Returns and refunds

- Returns are accepted within the published policy window.
- Electronics returns require a reason code and a quality check.
- A return rate above 5% for a category requires a root-cause review.
- Refund delays are reported as a service failure, not only as a finance processing issue.

## 5. Inventory and supply chain

### 5.1 Replenishment

- Every SKU–warehouse line has a safety stock and reorder point based on demand, lead time, and service target.
- When on-hand plus on-order inventory is below the reorder point, the replenishment owner must act within one working day.
- Expedited freight requires a documented business case and the appropriate approval.
- Inventory transfers between DCs require a cost-benefit check, including handling and transport.

### 5.2 Overstock and markdown

- Stock above 9 weeks of supply is treated as overstock.
- Seasonal overstock must have a clearance plan before the end of the season.
- Markdowns follow the decision-rights table.
- Markdown decisions must consider margin, cash, storage cost, and brand impact.

### 5.3 Inventory accuracy

- Cycle counts are performed on a rotating schedule.
- A SKU with a count variance above 2% is investigated.
- Master-data ownership must be explicit. If ownership is unclear, the COO and CIO must resolve it.
- Inventory reports used in management decisions must state their as-of date.

## 6. Financial control and procurement

- Purchase orders are required before goods or services are committed.
- Three quotes are required for purchases above $100,000, unless a sole-source exception is approved.
- Budget owners review actuals monthly and explain variances above 5%.
- Professional-services engagements above $250,000 require CFO approval.
- Capital projects above $1,000,000 require a stage-gate review before the next funding release.
- No employee may approve a transaction in which they have a personal interest.

## 7. Technology, data, and information

- Company data is classified as public, internal, confidential, or restricted.
- Employee, customer, and financial data must be handled according to classification.
- Access is granted on the principle of least privilege.
- A system change that affects inventory, pricing, or customer data requires testing and a rollback plan.
- Data used in management reports must have a named owner and a defined refresh cycle.
