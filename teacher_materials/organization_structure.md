The Executive Committee consists of the CEO, CFO, COO, CHRO, CMO, CIO, and General Counsel. The VP Sales and VP Customer Support attend the monthly operating review.

## 3. Regional and distribution layer

| Region | Distribution centre | Commercial coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North | North DC | Highest revenue share; largest Q3 decline |
| South | South DC | Most stable Q3 performance |
| East | East DC | Strong e-commerce; electronics availability risk |
| West | West DC | Seasonal overstock concentration |

Regional directors coordinate commercial execution across channels. DC managers report to the VP Operations for fulfilment, inventory accuracy, labour, and safety. Store managers report through the regional operations structure. The matrix is intended to combine functional expertise with regional responsiveness, but it creates dual-reporting tension at the demand-planning and customer-promise hand-offs.

## 4. Team and span profile

The employee extract records an average team size of 10.0 employees (median 10, range 1–19). Team size by itself does not establish whether a span is too wide or too narrow. Any recommendation to add, remove, or consolidate management layers should use workload, service, cost, and performance evidence rather than a single structural measure.

## 6. Current structural issues

1. **Inventory master-data ownership is unclear.** The inventory data cleanup task (T-020) is blocked and past due. Operations and IT both depend on the data but neither has end-to-end accountability.
2. **Demand planning is functionally fragmented.** Sales forecasts, marketing promotions, and operations replenishment are not consistently reconciled before commitments are made.
3. **Customer promises cross too many boundaries.** Customer Support commits to customers, Operations fulfils, and Logistics arranges delivery; no single role owns the end-to-end promise.
4. **Project governance is slow.** Three tasks are blocked and one is past due. The escalation route for cross-functional blockers is not being used consistently.
5. **Performance measurement is not differentiating.** Ratings are almost uniform, which weakens staffing, promotion, and development decisions.
6. **The regional-functional matrix creates ambiguity.** Regional directors and functional VPs both have legitimate claims on priorities, but decision rights at the hand-off points are not always explicit.

## 7. Structural implications for management

- Clarify accountability for inventory data and end-to-end customer promises.
- Establish a single demand plan that Sales, Marketing, Operations, and Finance sign up to.
- Use the existing S&OP and MOR forums rather than creating parallel meetings.
- Give cross-functional task owners explicit decision rights and escalation routes.
- Calibrate performance ratings and strengthen first-line manager capability.
- Review spans and layers in operations and customer support only where the workload evidence supports it; do not assume that delayering is automatically the answer.
