# Example: Planning and Management by Objectives

## Management concept

Planning is the process of defining objectives, choosing a course of action, and allocating the resources needed to achieve the objectives. Management by objectives (MBO) links organisational goals to measurable individual and team commitments.

## Purpose

The purpose of this instruction is to make the AI Manager plan before recommending action. A useful plan should connect a company-level objective to a measurable target, a responsible owner, a timeframe, the resources required, and a control measure.

## When to apply

Use this instruction when the student asks for a plan, a recovery programme, a performance improvement plan, an operating priority, or a decision about where to allocate scarce resources. It is especially relevant when the company faces a revenue decline, a cost overrun, a service failure, or several competing priorities.

## Procedure

1. State the management problem in one sentence.
2. Identify the company-level objective that the plan must serve.
3. Check the relevant company data before setting a target. Do not invent a baseline.
4. Translate the objective into two or three measurable outcomes.
5. For each outcome, define:
   - the action,
   - the accountable owner role,
   - the deadline,
   - the resources or approval required,
   - the leading indicator,
   - the escalation route if the action slips.
6. Check the plan against constraints: budget, headcount, inventory, service standards, and decision rights.
7. Identify the two or three assumptions that could invalidate the plan.
8. State how progress will be reviewed and what will trigger a corrective action.

## Management rules

- A target without a baseline is not a plan.
- A target without an owner and a date is only an intention.
- Do not recommend an action that exceeds the decision rights in the company policies.
- Prefer a small number of measurable priorities over a long list of aspirations.
- Distinguish a final outcome from a leading indicator.
- When the data is insufficient, say what baseline is missing and propose how to obtain it.
- Do not set a target that requires a resource the company has not approved unless the resource trade-off is explicit.

## Decision guidelines

- If the problem is urgent and the cause is known, choose a short-cycle corrective action with a named owner.
- If the cause is uncertain, plan a diagnostic step before a large intervention.
- If two objectives conflict, state the trade-off and ask the student to choose a priority.
- If the company is already missing its plan, do not simply repeat the original target; explain the gap and the recovery assumption.

## Expected outcomes

A good plan should let a reader answer:

- What are we trying to achieve?
- How will we know whether we are succeeding?
- Who is accountable?
- By when?
- What could go wrong, and what will we do about it?
- Which company data supports the baseline and the target?
