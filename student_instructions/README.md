# Student Management Instructions

This folder is yours. Write the management knowledge, procedures, and decision rules you want your AI Manager to apply to the common company case.

## What belongs here

Each `.md` file should express **your** understanding of a management concept and how it should be used in practice. A useful file often contains:

- The management concept and why it matters.
- The objectives the concept should achieve.
- The situations in which it applies.
- A procedure or sequence of steps.
- Management rules and decision guidelines.
- Expected outcomes or indicators of success.
- The company data or evidence the AI Manager should check.

You do not have to follow that structure. It is a thinking aid, not a required template. You may write one broad file or many focused files.

## Suggested topics

Planning, organizing, staffing, leading, motivation, delegation, communication, coordination, decision making, controlling, performance evaluation, change management, team development, conflict resolution, operational control, and management by objectives.

These are examples. Use the course concepts you have studied and explain them in your own words.

## Naming and discovery

- Active instruction files must end in `.md`.
- Use descriptive names such as `planning.md`, `delegation_rules.md`, or `performance_control.md`.
- Files and folders starting with `_` are ignored by the AI Manager and excluded from the submission ZIP. This is where optional examples live.
- `README.md` is ignored.
- Subfolders are supported. For example `planning/goal_setting.md` is active.
- Do not put teacher company data in this folder. It belongs in `teacher_materials/`.

## Optional examples

The `_examples` folder contains examples to help you get started. They are deliberately inactive. Read them, then write your own file in your own words. If you copy an example, revise it substantially and make it specific to your management approach.

## Optional built-in skills

The `builtin_skills/` folder contains optional project-management methods such as Gantt planning, OKRs, stakeholder mapping, risk registers, and operating reviews. They are **not** part of your instruction files and are **not** included in the submission ZIP. The AI Manager reads a built-in skill only when you explicitly invoke it by name, for example:

```text
Use the /gantt-planner skill to schedule the inventory recovery plan.
```

See the app's **Help → Built-in project-management skills** section for the catalog and invocation examples. If you want a skill method to apply permanently, adapt it into your own instruction file.

## Academic integrity

- Your instruction files must be your own work.
- Do not submit an example unchanged as your own management thinking.
- The AI Manager session log records the files used for each response. Your PDF report makes this traceable.
- If you use a concept from the course, explain it in your own words rather than copying lecture slides.

## How to use the files

1. Write or edit your `.md` files in the app's **Instructions** page, or with any text editor.
2. Start a session in the **AI Manager** page.
3. Give your AI Manager the session briefing. It will read these files and the teacher data.
4. Ask management questions and follow up. The AI Manager should cite your files and the company data.
5. Log each turn. The session PDF will show the exact queries, responses, instructions used, company data consulted, evidence, and principles.
6. Export the PDF and the ZIP of your active instruction files.

## What the AI Manager is told

The environment protocol requires the AI Manager to:

- Prioritise your instruction files over generic management advice.
- Use the teacher-provided company data for factual claims.
- Cite the files and figures it uses.
- Never invent company information.
- Keep the conversation context across turns.
- Log the exact query and full response.
- Avoid exposing private chain-of-thought; give concise analysis and recommendations.

Your instructions should make it clear what you want the AI Manager to do when the company data is incomplete, conflicting, or outside your stated rules.
