# OKR Strategist

**Invocation rule:** Apply this skill only when the student explicitly invokes it or asks for OKRs, objectives and key results, goal alignment, or an OKR review. Do not auto-apply it to every planning or performance question.

## Purpose

Help the student translate a management priority into a small set of meaningful objectives and measurable key results. The skill also tests whether the OKRs are aligned, achievable, and reviewable.

## Inputs

Gather:

- the strategic or operating objective,
- the current baseline from company data,
- the time period,
- the accountable owner roles,
- the constraints and dependencies,
- the existing goals or scorecard.

Use `sales.csv`, `expenses.csv`, `inventory.csv`, `customer_feedback.csv`, `headcount_plan.csv`, and `tasks.csv` where relevant. Do not invent a baseline.

## Procedure

1. **Clarify the outcome.** State what should be different at the end of the period and why it matters.
2. **Draft objectives.** Write two or three qualitative, motivating objectives. Avoid activities and output counts.
3. **Draft key results.** For each objective, write two to four measurable key results. Each must have a baseline, target, owner, and measurement source.
4. **Test quality.** Check whether each key result is:
   - outcome-oriented, not just activity,
   - measurable and time-bound,
   - within the team's influence,
   - supported by a baseline,
   - balanced between leading and lagging measures,
   - not encouraging harmful behaviour.
5. **Check alignment.** Show how the objectives connect to the company priorities and to functional or team goals.
6. **Define the review.** State the weekly or monthly check-in, the confidence rating, and the action trigger if progress is off track.
7. **Identify risks.** Note dependencies, measurement gaps, and trade-offs.

## Output format

Provide:

- an OKR table with objective, key result, baseline, target, owner, source, and confidence,
- an alignment map,
- a review cadence,
- quality issues and recommended revisions,
- assumptions.

## Quality checks

- No key result is just "complete a project" unless completion is the actual outcome.
- Baselines come from company data or are explicitly marked as missing.
- Targets are ambitious but not detached from capacity.
- Owners are roles, not invented names.
- The OKRs do not conflict with company policy or the student's instructions.
- The review cadence is realistic.
