# Stakeholder Map

**Invocation rule:** Apply this skill only when the student explicitly invokes it or asks for stakeholder analysis, an engagement plan, or a communication strategy. Do not auto-apply it to every management question.

## Purpose

Help a top-level manager understand who matters, who can block or accelerate the work, and how to engage each group. The map connects analysis to a practical communication and engagement plan.

## Inputs

Gather:

- the decision, project, or change,
- the stakeholder groups and roles,
- their interest, influence, and current attitude,
- the impact on each group,
- the decision rights and governance forums,
- the timeline.

Use `organization_structure.md`, `company_policies.md`, and the management committee minutes. Do not invent names or personal positions.

## Procedure

1. **Define the change.** State what is being decided or implemented and why.
2. **Identify stakeholders.** Include employees, managers, executives, customers, suppliers, partners, regulators, and communities where relevant.
3. **Assess each group.** Rate:
   - interest in the outcome,
   - influence over the decision or implementation,
   - current attitude: supportive, neutral, resistant, unknown,
   - impact: high, medium, low.
4. **Map priority.** High influence and high interest need active partnership. High influence and low interest need consultation and concise updates. Low influence and high interest need information and voice. Low influence and low interest need monitoring.
5. **Plan engagement.** For each priority group, state:
   - the objective,
   - the message,
   - the channel,
   - the owner,
   - the timing,
   - the feedback mechanism.
6. **Address resistance.** Identify the likely source of resistance and whether it is about interests, information, capability, or trust. Do not label resistance as irrational without evidence.
7. **Monitor.** Define the signals that show engagement is working or failing, and the escalation route.

## Output format

Provide:

- a stakeholder table with group, interest, influence, attitude, impact, and engagement approach,
- a communication plan,
- key risks and resistance,
- feedback and review measures.

## Quality checks

- Stakeholders are roles or groups, not invented individuals.
- Influence and interest are justified.
- The plan distinguishes inform, consult, involve, and partner.
- Communication is audience-specific and honest.
- The student's instructions and company evidence remain authoritative.
