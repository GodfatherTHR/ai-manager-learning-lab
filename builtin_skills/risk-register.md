# Risk Register

**Invocation rule:** Apply this skill only when the student explicitly invokes it or asks for a risk register, risk assessment, mitigation plan, or control review. Do not auto-apply it to every recommendation.

## Purpose

Make risks visible and owned. The skill helps a manager distinguish a cause, an uncertain event, and its impact, then assign a proportionate response.

## Inputs

Gather:

- the decision, project, or operating area,
- the objectives and constraints,
- the available company evidence,
- the company policies and risk thresholds,
- the relevant stakeholders and owners,
- the review cycle.

## Procedure

1. **Set the context.** State the objective, scope, and risk appetite or policy threshold.
2. **Identify risks.** Use the categories: strategic, financial, operational, customer, people, technology, legal, compliance, supplier, and reputational. Include opportunities where relevant.
3. **Write each risk clearly.** Use the form: cause → uncertain event → impact. Avoid vague labels such as "poor communication".
4. **Assess.** Rate likelihood and impact as high, medium, or low. Use a simple score or matrix. Explain the basis.
5. **Assign an owner.** Every risk needs a role accountable for monitoring and response.
6. **Choose a response.** Consider avoid, reduce, transfer, accept, or exploit (for opportunities). State the mitigation and the contingency.
7. **Define a trigger.** State the leading indicator or event that activates the contingency or escalation.
8. **Review.** Set the review cadence and the escalation route.

## Output format

Provide:

- a risk register with ID, risk, cause, impact, likelihood, score, owner, mitigation, contingency, trigger, and review date,
- the top three risks,
- risk appetite and policy notes,
- residual risk after mitigation,
- assumptions.

## Quality checks

- Risks are specific and testable.
- Likelihood and impact are justified.
- Every high risk has an owner and a response.
- Mitigation is not confused with contingency.
- The register does not invent facts or present analysis as certainty.
- The student's instructions and company policies take precedence.
