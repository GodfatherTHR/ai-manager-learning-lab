# Work Recap Writer

**Invocation rule:** Apply this skill only when the student explicitly invokes it or asks for a weekly report, monthly recap, status update, sprint summary, or OKR progress report. Do not auto-apply it to every question.

## Purpose

Convert scattered notes and session history into a clear management recap. The recap should show what was done, what changed, what was decided, what is at risk, and what happens next.

## Inputs

Gather:

- the period covered,
- the audience,
- the session turns or notes,
- completed and in-progress tasks,
- decisions and approvals,
- metrics and evidence,
- risks and blockers,
- next priorities.

Use the session log, `tasks.csv`, and the relevant company data. Do not claim an achievement that is not supported by the record.

## Procedure

1. **Set the frame.** State the period, audience, and purpose.
2. **Collect the facts.** List completed work, decisions, metrics, and open items with sources.
3. **Separate output from outcome.** Distinguish activity from the result it produced.
4. **Choose the style.** Use one of:
   - **data-driven:** metrics, variances, and trends,
   - **narrative:** what happened, why it matters, what is next,
   - **OKR-aligned:** progress against objectives and key results.
5. **Highlight decisions.** State what was decided, by whom, and what changes as a result.
6. **Show risks and blockers.** Include owner and next action.
7. **Set the next period.** List the top three priorities and the decisions needed.
8. **Keep it honest.** Do not round away bad news or present plans as achievements.

## Output format

Provide:

- a one-paragraph executive summary,
- key results and metrics,
- decisions,
- risks and blockers,
- next priorities,
- a short action table with owner and date.

## Quality checks

- Every metric has a source and period.
- Completed work is distinguished from work in progress.
- Decisions are separated from discussion.
- Risks are not hidden.
- The recap does not invent progress, names, or dates.
- The student's instructions and company evidence remain authoritative.
