# Project Discovery

**Invocation rule:** Apply this skill only when the student explicitly invokes it or asks for project discovery, a project brief, a PRD-style document, customer or employee research, or a launch plan. Do not auto-apply it to every question.

## Purpose

Prevent the team from building the wrong solution. The skill moves from a problem and its users to testable assumptions, a small experiment, a minimum viable solution, and a launch brief.

## Inputs

Gather:

- the problem or opportunity,
- the affected users or stakeholders,
- the evidence already available,
- the constraints,
- the decision owner,
- the time and budget available.

Use company data and documents. Do not invent customer research or employee feedback.

## Procedure

1. **Research.** Summarize what is known and what is missing. Use the company data, documents, and session history.
2. **Define the problem.** Write a problem statement with the affected group, the need, and the evidence. Avoid jumping to a solution.
3. **Map the job to be done.** Describe what the user is trying to achieve, the current alternatives, and the pain points.
4. **State assumptions.** List the assumptions that must be true for the project to work. Rank them by importance and uncertainty.
5. **Design validation.** Propose the smallest experiment or diagnostic that would test the riskiest assumption. State the success criterion.
6. **Define the minimum solution.** Describe the smallest useful deliverable, the scope, the non-goals, and the acceptance criteria.
7. **Plan the launch.** State the audience, readiness checks, communication, support, and measures.
8. **Decide.** Recommend proceed, test, pause, or stop, with the evidence and the decision owner.

## Output format

Provide:

- a discovery brief with problem, users, evidence, and gaps,
- an assumption table with test and success criterion,
- a minimum-solution scope and non-goals,
- a launch checklist,
- a recommendation and decision owner.

## Quality checks

- The problem is not replaced by a preferred solution.
- Assumptions are explicit.
- The evidence is cited.
- The experiment is small, feasible, and measurable.
- Launch readiness includes support, communication, and a rollback.
- External tools are not assumed; local Markdown is sufficient.
