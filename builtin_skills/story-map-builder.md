# Story Map Builder

**Invocation rule:** Apply this skill only when the student explicitly invokes it or asks for a story map, requirement map, backlog organization, MoSCoW prioritization, or release slicing. Do not auto-apply it to every planning question.

## Purpose

Turn a large set of requirements into a visible map from user or management outcome to deliverable to task. The map helps the student see what belongs together, what can wait, and what should be released first.

## Inputs

Gather:

- the customer, employee, or management outcome,
- the requirements or backlog items,
- the users or stakeholder groups,
- constraints and dependencies,
- the delivery horizon.

Use company documents and session decisions. Do not invent customer requirements or employee feedback.

## Procedure

1. **Define the outcome.** State the change the user or organisation needs.
2. **Build the backbone.** Identify the main stages or activities in the user journey or management process.
3. **Add deliverables and tasks.** Under each stage, list the deliverables and the tasks needed to produce them.
4. **Prioritize.** Use MoSCoW:
   - Must have,
   - Should have,
   - Could have,
   - Will not have now.
5. **Slice releases.** Group items into a first release, second release, and later. Ensure each slice delivers a usable outcome.
6. **Check dependencies.** Flag items that must precede others.
7. **Test the map.** Ask whether the first slice solves a meaningful problem and whether any "must" is actually optional.
8. **Summarize the trade-offs.** State what is being deferred and why.

## Output format

Provide:

- a story-map table with outcome, stage, deliverable, task, priority, release, and dependency,
- a first-release summary,
- deferred items and rationale,
- risks and assumptions.

A Mermaid diagram or Markdown table is sufficient. No external backlog tool is required.

## Quality checks

- The map starts from an outcome, not a feature list.
- Priorities are explicit.
- The first release is coherent and usable.
- Deferred items are not hidden.
- Dependencies are visible.
- The student's instructions and company evidence remain authoritative.
